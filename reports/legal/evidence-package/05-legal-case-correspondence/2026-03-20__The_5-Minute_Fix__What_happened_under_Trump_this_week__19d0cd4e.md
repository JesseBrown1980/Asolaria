Yes, keep this newsletter By Amber Phillips Here’s what happened under President Donald Trump this week, some of which you may have missed. He’s struggling to bring down gas prices As Iran bombs energy fields across the Middle East, airfare is getting more expensive , the Federal Reserve is worried about inflation and even the oil companies could suffer . There is not much Trump can meaningfully do about this, economists say. His administration has released American reserves of oil and tried to bring in more from foes such as Russia and now maybe even Iran . All of this so far hasn’t put much of a dent in the wartime spike, as Iran shows a continued strength to fight that may be surprising U.S. officials. “The president could maybe move gas prices down,” said Michael Strain, an economist with the conservative-leaning American Enterprise Institute. “But not by nearly as much as the price of oil has pushed them up.” Trump asserted he can take Cuba “I can do anything I want with it,” he said this week. After arresting Venezuela’s president and essentially taking over the South American nation, Trump set his sights on Cuba. The U.S. has blocked fuel from entering Cuba, and the island’s already struggling energy grid collapsed Monday. It’s not clear whether Trump could just take over Cuba. There are several barriers standing in Trump’s way. The U.S. relationship with Cuba is actually codified in law, which means Trump couldn’t, for example, lift a long-standing U.S. trade embargo on Cuba without an act of Congress, said William Alan Reinsch, a trade expert who now advises the Center for Strategic and International Studies. The law also says that the U.S. can’t have diplomatic relations with Cuba until it settles billions of dollars of outstanding property claims from U.S. citizens and companies stemming from when Cuban leader Fidel Castro seized much of the island in the 1960s. Advertisement “Another issue is what does the U.S. government actually want in Cuba?” Reinsch said, pointing out it is not an island rich with resources. “You have 60-plus years of a socialist regime that until recently suppressed virtually all private enterprise. Taking responsibility for its recovery would cost an enormous amount of money.” Trump’s ballroom is back in court After knocking down the East Wing of the White House last year, Trump is rushing to build an enormous ballroom in its place starting in April. But a federal judge is considering pausing construction. Trump has stressed that taxpayers aren’t paying for the ballroom, and he says he can make whatever changes he wants to the White House because he’s president. In a hearing this week, U.S. District Judge Richard J. Leon said Trump is a “steward” of the White House, not the owner of it. He seemed skeptical at multiple points of Trump’s justification of the ballroom project, my Washington Post colleagues report. At issue seems to be that Trump didn’t get congressional approval (Congress has authority over federal properties) and if Trump is relying on private donations to bypass Congress. Advertisement “The Defendants have no authority to build the Ballroom — not even close,” lawyers for National Trust for Historic Preservation, a nonprofit charged by Congress with preserving historic buildings argued. A judge threw the case out last month on procedural issues, but the trust amended its lawsuit. There may also be a court challenge on whether Trump legally appointed loyalists to the arts commission evaluating the ballroom. And separate from knocking down the East Wing, a Trump ally who leads another arts commission, wants to replace White House columns with a style Trump prefers. Most immigrants can no longer be truck drivers Around 200,000 truck drivers will lose their commercial licenses starting this week as a rule prohibiting most immigrants from the job takes effect. Immigrants make up a growing portion of the field, which transports goods across America. “With long hours, low pay, dangerous road conditions and extended periods away from home, trucking is plagued by high turnover rates,” The Post’s Lauren Kaori Gurley reports. “As Americans have left the industry, immigrants have moved in amid worsening working conditions and deregulation.” There were several high-profile accidents last year, and the Trump administration now bars asylum seekers, refugees or recipients of Deferred Action for Childhood Arrivals from having a commercial license. Some drivers are suing the Trump administration alleging discrimination. Here’s what happened under President Donald Trump this week, some of which you may have missed.
He’s struggling to bring down gas prices
As Iran bombs energy fields across the Middle East, airfare is getting more expensive <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/471090a/69bda69f8c1e360585f2f9da/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/13/53/69bda69f8c1e360585f2f9da>, the Federal Reserve is worried about inflation <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/471090b/69bda69f8c1e360585f2f9da/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/14/53/69bda69f8c1e360585f2f9da> and even the oil companies could suffer <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/471090c/69bda69f8c1e360585f2f9da/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/15/53/69bda69f8c1e360585f2f9da>.

There is not much Trump can meaningfully do about this, economists say. His administration has released American reserves of oil and tried to bring in more from foes such as Russia and now maybe even Iran <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/471090d/69bda69f8c1e360585f2f9da/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/16/53/69bda69f8c1e360585f2f9da>. All of this so far hasn’t put much of a dent in the wartime spike, as Iran shows a continued strength to fight that may be surprising U.S. officials.

<https://sli.washingtonpost.com/click?s=987489&li=thefix&m=629a290b99188f8d03d630cf68dfeaec&p=69bda69f8c1e360585f2f9da&stpe=default>

<https://sli.washingtonpost.com/click?s=987489&li=thefix&m=629a290b99188f8d03d630cf68dfeaec&p=69bda69f8c1e360585f2f9da&stpe=default>

“The president could maybe move gas prices down,” said Michael Strain, an economist with the conservative-leaning American Enterprise Institute. “But not by nearly as much as the price of oil has pushed them up.”
Trump asserted he can take Cuba
“I can do anything I want with it,” he said this week. After arresting Venezuela’s president and essentially taking over the South American nation, Trump set his sights on Cuba. The U.S. has blocked fuel from entering Cuba, and the island’s already struggling energy grid collapsed <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/471090e/69bda69f8c1e360585f2f9da/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/19/53/69bda69f8c1e360585f2f9da> Monday.

It’s not clear whether Trump could just take over Cuba. There are several barriers standing in Trump’s way. The U.S. relationship with Cuba is actually codified in law, which means Trump couldn’t, for example, lift a long-standing U.S. trade embargo on Cuba without an act of Congress, said William Alan Reinsch, a trade expert who now advises the Center for Strategic and International Studies.

The law also says that the U.S. can’t have diplomatic relations with Cuba until it settles billions of dollars of outstanding property claims from U.S. citizens and companies stemming from when Cuban leader Fidel Castro seized much of the island in the 1960s.

“Another issue is what does the U.S. government actually want in Cuba?” Reinsch said, pointing out it is not an island rich with resources. “You have 60-plus years of a socialist regime that until recently suppressed virtually all private enterprise. Taking responsibility for its recovery would cost an enormous amount of money.”
Trump’s ballroom is back in court
After knocking down the East Wing of the White House last year, Trump is rushing to build an enormous ballroom in its place starting in April. But a federal judge is considering pausing construction.

Trump has stressed that taxpayers aren’t paying for the ballroom, and he says he can make whatever changes he wants to the White House because he’s president.

In a hearing this week, U.S. District Judge Richard J. Leon said Trump is a “steward” of the White House, not the owner of it. He seemed skeptical <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/471090f/69bda69f8c1e360585f2f9da/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/21/53/69bda69f8c1e360585f2f9da> at multiple points of Trump’s justification of the ballroom project, my Washington Post colleagues report. At issue seems to be that Trump didn’t get congressional approval (Congress has authority over federal properties) and if Trump is relying  <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/46b6b32/6996325a52fff236344bc2ae/596a0e109bbc0f6d71c91171/29/57/6996325a52fff236344bc2ae?utm_campaign=wp_the_5_minute_fix&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter>on private donations to bypass Congress.

“The Defendants have no authority to build the Ballroom — not even close,” lawyers for National Trust for Historic Preservation, a nonprofit charged by Congress with preserving historic buildings argued.

A judge threw the case out last month on procedural issues, but the trust amended its lawsuit.

There may also be a court challenge <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/4710910/69bda69f8c1e360585f2f9da/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/24/53/69bda69f8c1e360585f2f9da> on whether Trump legally appointed loyalists to the arts commission evaluating the ballroom. And separate from knocking down the East Wing, a Trump ally who leads another arts commission, wants to replace <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/470610b/69bda69f8c1e360585f2f9da/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/25/53/69bda69f8c1e360585f2f9da> White House columns with a style Trump prefers.
Most immigrants can no longer be truck drivers
Around 200,000 truck drivers will lose their commercial licenses starting this week as a rule prohibiting most immigrants  <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/4710911/69bda69f8c1e360585f2f9da/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/26/53/69bda69f8c1e360585f2f9da>from the job takes effect.

Immigrants make up a growing portion of the field, which transports goods across America. “With long hours, low pay, dangerous road conditions and extended periods away from home, trucking is plagued by high turnover rates,” The Post’s Lauren Kaori Gurley <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/4221251/69bda69f8c1e360585f2f9da/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/27/53/69bda69f8c1e360585f2f9da> reports. “As Americans have left the industry, immigrants have moved in amid worsening working conditions and deregulation.”

There were several high-profile accidents last year, and the Trump administration now bars asylum seekers, refugees or recipients of Deferred Action for Childhood Arrivals from having a commercial license. Some drivers are suing the Trump administration alleging discrimination.
