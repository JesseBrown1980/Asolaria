Republicans ramp up their anti-Muslim rhetoric Raising fears of Muslims animated President Donald Trump’s ascension to power more than a decade ago. The practice seems to be on the rise again, this time elevated by Republican members of Congress, some of whom who have been offering blatantly Islamophobic statements. “Muslims don’t belong in American society,” one Republican member of Congress, Rep. Andrew Ogles (Tennessee), posted on social media this week. “The enemy is inside the gates,” Sen. Tommy Tuberville (Alabama) posted this week, with an illustration seemingly designed to align New York City’s mayor, who is Muslim, with the Sept. 11, 2001, terrorist attacks in New York. It’s not clear what led to these posts — other than possibly the United States being at war with Iran, a Muslim country. But since his election in November, the liberal New York Mayor Zohran Mamdani (D) has become a target for Republicans across the country who are trying to make him a foil for the midterm elections. His politics are so far to the left that the top two Democrats in Congress, who are also from New York, hesitated to endorse him or didn’t. My Washington Post colleagues find that since the beginning of the year, nearly 100 GOP members of Congress have posted about Islam or Muslims, and almost all of those posts have been negative. There’s still a shutdown over immigration The Department of Homeland Security has been shut down for almost a month. Advertisement It started after Trump’s unpopular immigration surge in Minneapolis that detained thousands , most of whom don’t have criminal records and many of whom have been in the country for more than a decade. Two U.S. citizens were shot to death by immigration agents in January during the surge. Congress was on the verge of passing on a spending bill at the time, and Democrats demanded changes to how immigration agents conduct their raids. Even though there has been bipartisan criticism for some of ICE agents’ tactics, Congress couldn’t agree and left the agency in charge of immigration, the Department of Homeland Security, unfunded. The rest of the government is funded through September. Initially, Democrats felt like they had the political upper hand. Much of the violence had been caught on video, helping turn Trump’s previously most popular issue, immigration, into a liability. It’s unclear if that’s still the case. DHS also oversees the Coast Guard, disaster management agencies and airport security. The shutdown is leading to some are flight delays as Transportation Security Administration workers miss paychecks. (Immigration and Customs Enforcement has been operating as if there was no shutdown because congressional Republicans infused it with $75 billion in previous legislation.) Other than more widespread airport delays or a natural disaster that the government can’t respond to as effectively, it’s hard to see what could end this partial shutdown for some of the nation’s most critical agencies. Advertisement The federal government is hiring again — with a catch It’s been a year since Elon Musk and his U.S. DOGE Service fired hundreds of thousands of federal workers and gained access to extremely sensitive data in the process. Now the Trump administration is hiring again. “We probably have some skills that we now need to hire back, quite frankly,” Scott Kupor, the head of the Office of Personnel Management, said in an interview with my colleagues at The Washington Post . “There’s no question anytime you do restructurings … sometimes you over-restructure, sometimes you under-restructure.” The Trump administration is trying to rehire people in a way that makes it easier to fire workers who aren’t aligned with Trump’s priorities. Replacing the federal workforce with loyalists was a major part of Project 2025, a plan developed by Trump loyalists for his second administration. These technical changes to governance can go under the radar but strengthen Trump by making a more powerful president, says Kimberly Lane Scheppele, who studies the rise and fall of constitutional governments at Princeton University. She compared it to how Hungary’s president turned the country into an authoritarian one. “It’s the legal vector for how to entrench Trump in power,” she said. The Trump administration is pausing some of its vaccine rollbacks Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. has remade the nation’s health department to push back on vaccines that scientists and public health experts have said save lives. Under his leadership, the Trump administration has considered blocking vaccines using a special technology known as mRNA, which helped create the covid-19 vaccine in record time and now some vaccine companies are using for flu shots. And the government no longer recommends a number of vaccines designed to keep infants and children safe. But there are signs health officials are hitting pause on plans to do more as midterm election loom this year. This month, a key federal vaccine advisory panel abandoned a plan to stop recommending mRNA vaccines altogether, The Washington Post’s Lena H. Sun and Rachel Roubein report. They also report that vaccines are still largely popular with Americans and some Republicans are warning that their party could lose in the midterm elections if this becomes a bigger issue. Here’s what happened under the Trump administration this week.
Republicans ramp up their anti-Muslim rhetoric
Raising fears of Muslims animated President Donald Trump’s ascension to power more than a decade ago. The practice seems to be on the rise again, this time elevated by Republican members of Congress, some of whom who have been offering blatantly Islamophobic statements.

“Muslims don’t belong in American society,” one Republican member of Congress, Rep. Andrew Ogles (Tennessee), posted <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/46f5e5f/69b46cc45bb15c314769afc4/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/13/49/69b46cc45bb15c314769afc4> on social media this week.

<https://sli.washingtonpost.com/click?s=987489&li=thefix&m=629a290b99188f8d03d630cf68dfeaec&p=69b46cc45bb15c314769afc4&stpe=default>

<https://sli.washingtonpost.com/click?s=987489&li=thefix&m=629a290b99188f8d03d630cf68dfeaec&p=69b46cc45bb15c314769afc4&stpe=default>

“The enemy is inside the gates,” Sen. Tommy Tuberville (Alabama) posted <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/46ff102/69b46cc45bb15c314769afc4/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/16/49/69b46cc45bb15c314769afc4> this week, with an illustration seemingly designed to align New York City’s mayor, who is Muslim, with the Sept. 11, 2001, terrorist attacks in New York.

It’s not clear what led to these posts — other than possibly the United States being at war with Iran, a Muslim country. But since his election in November, the liberal New York Mayor Zohran Mamdani (D) has become a target for Republicans across the country who are trying to make him a foil for the midterm elections. His politics are so far to the left that the top two Democrats in Congress, who are also from New York, hesitated to endorse him or didn’t.

My Washington Post colleagues find <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/46ff102/69b46cc45bb15c314769afc4/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/17/49/69b46cc45bb15c314769afc4> that since the beginning of the year, nearly 100 GOP members of Congress have posted about Islam or Muslims, and almost all of those posts have been negative.
There’s still a shutdown over immigration
The Department of Homeland Security has been shut down <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/46f7c95/69b46cc45bb15c314769afc4/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/18/49/69b46cc45bb15c314769afc4> for almost a month.

It started after Trump’s unpopular immigration surge in Minneapolis that detained thousands <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/46ff103/69b46cc45bb15c314769afc4/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/20/49/69b46cc45bb15c314769afc4>, most of whom don’t have criminal records and many of whom have been in the country for more than a decade. Two U.S. citizens were shot to death by immigration agents in January during the surge.

Congress was on the verge of passing on a spending bill at the time, and Democrats demanded changes to how immigration agents conduct their raids. Even though there has been bipartisan criticism for some of ICE agents’ tactics, Congress couldn’t agree and left the agency in charge of immigration, the Department of Homeland Security, unfunded. The rest of the government is funded through September.

Initially, Democrats felt like they had the political upper hand. Much of the violence had been caught on video, helping turn Trump’s previously most popular issue, immigration, into a liability.

It’s unclear if that’s still the case. DHS also oversees the Coast Guard, disaster management agencies and airport security. The shutdown is leading to some are flight delays as Transportation Security Administration workers miss paychecks. (Immigration and Customs Enforcement has been operating as if there was no shutdown because congressional Republicans infused it with $75 billion <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/469a329/69b46cc45bb15c314769afc4/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/21/49/69b46cc45bb15c314769afc4> in previous legislation.)

Other than more widespread airport delays or a natural disaster that the government can’t respond to as effectively, it’s hard to see what could end this partial shutdown for some of the nation’s most critical agencies.

The federal government is hiring again — with a catch
It’s been a year since Elon Musk and his U.S. DOGE Service fired hundreds of thousands of federal workers and gained access to extremely sensitive data <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/468b5a6/69b46cc45bb15c314769afc4/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/23/49/69b46cc45bb15c314769afc4> in the process. Now the Trump administration is hiring again.

“We probably have some skills that we now need to hire back, quite frankly,” Scott Kupor, the head of the Office of Personnel Management, said in an interview with my colleagues at The Washington Post <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/46ff104/69b46cc45bb15c314769afc4/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/24/49/69b46cc45bb15c314769afc4>. “There’s no question anytime you do restructurings … sometimes you over-restructure, sometimes you under-restructure.”

The Trump administration is trying to rehire people in a way that makes it easier to fire workers who aren’t aligned with Trump’s priorities.

Replacing the federal workforce with loyalists was a major part of Project 2025, a plan developed by Trump loyalists for his second administration.

These technical changes to governance can go under the radar but strengthen Trump by making a more powerful president, says Kimberly Lane Scheppele, who studies the rise and fall of constitutional governments at Princeton University. She compared it to how Hungary’s president turned the country into an authoritarian one. “It’s the legal vector for how to entrench Trump in power,” she said.
The Trump administration is pausing some of its vaccine rollbacks
Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. has remade the nation’s health department to push back on vaccines that scientists and public health experts have said save lives.

Under his leadership, the Trump administration has considered blocking vaccines using a special technology known as mRNA, which helped create the covid-19 vaccine in record time and now some vaccine companies are using for flu shots. And the government no longer recommends <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/4660213/69b46cc45bb15c314769afc4/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/25/49/69b46cc45bb15c314769afc4> a number of vaccines designed to keep infants and children safe.

But there are signs health officials are hitting pause on plans to do more as midterm election loom this year. This month, a key federal vaccine advisory panel abandoned a plan <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/46fa1cf/69b46cc45bb15c314769afc4/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/26/49/69b46cc45bb15c314769afc4> to stop recommending mRNA vaccines altogether, The Washington Post’s Lena H. Sun <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/46ff105/69b46cc45bb15c314769afc4/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/27/49/69b46cc45bb15c314769afc4> and Rachel Roubein <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/418e971/69b46cc45bb15c314769afc4/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/28/49/69b46cc45bb15c314769afc4> report.

They also report that vaccines are still largely popular with Americans and some Republicans are warning <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/46ddca2/69b46cc45bb15c314769afc4/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/29/49/69b46cc45bb15c314769afc4> that their party could lose in the midterm elections if this becomes a bigger issue. Trump pollster Tony Fabrizio has warned that candidates who support ending vaccine requirements “will pay a political price.”
