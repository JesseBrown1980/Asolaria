Workplace trends

Work-life balance

Micro-retirement is the new way to beat burnout

Workers are taking extended unpaid breaks, lasting weeks or even
months, before returning to their jobs, a trend known as
micro-retirement. Unlike sabbaticals reserved for professors or
tech professionals, this movement has spread across all age
groups as people prioritize mental health over constant career
advancement, Fast Company reports. Gen Z, in particular, has
embraced the trend, seeking fulfillment and work-life balance
over climbing the corporate ladder. However, HR experts warn that
these frequent breaks can damage earnings, retirement savings,
and career growth, with some hiring managers viewing
microretirees as job hoppers in competitive fields. Despite these
warnings, as lifespans increase and retirement feels distant,
workers are seizing moments today rather than banking everything
on tomorrow.

Fraud

Job scams are exploding as hiring slows

Reports of online job scams jumped 19% in the first half of 2025
compared to last year, costing hopeful job-seekers almost $300
million, the Federal Trade Commission reports. Scammers pose as
recruiters or post fake offers on legitimate sites like LinkedIn
and ZipRecruiter, luring applicants to click malicious links that
steal personal information like Social Security numbers and bank
details. Text-based scams have surged particularly fast,
quadrupling from 4,872 reports in 2020 to 20,673 last year,
according to FTC data. These scams hit especially hard now, as
Americans face the worst October for layoffs since 2003,
according to consulting firm Challenger, Gray and Christmas. The
combination of sophisticated tactics and a weak labor market
creates the perfect conditions for fraud, labor economists warn.

The AI corner

Entertainment

OpenAI's Sora could challenge TikTok

Open AI’s Sora lets users create any video they can imagine with
simple text prompts, from a rapping chicken to Bob Ross painting
mountains on a Target wall. Unlike traditional social media
cluttered with influencer content and AI-generated posts trying
to pass as authentic, Sora sidesteps authenticity entirely by
making everything obviously artificial, Medium reports. The
appeal reveals a shift in what people want from social media: 48%
of teens now say traditional platforms harm their mental health,
according to Pew Research. By offering pure creative spectacle
without pretense of reality, Sora taps into the same storytelling
and drama that drives engagement on TikTok, but without the
exhausting pressure to distinguish real from fake.

AI chatbots spark debate over protecting adult users

Seven new lawsuits against OpenAI reveal that AI chatbot risks

outlandish theories the chatbot fed him, Politico reports. The
cases expose a major policy gap: while proposed legislation
focuses almost entirely on protecting children from AI
companions, adults remain largely unprotected. As lawmakers
struggle to write safety rules, they face a central question:
what duty of care do companies owe adult users who can legally
make their own choices, even dangerous ones?
