I see that you were able to get one half of the form notarized, however it is the top half that was missing and needed to be notarized. Please let us know if you have any further questions about the requirements of this process. Additionally, I am glad you emailed because there is one more document that I forgot to add to the list needed to complete your children's birth certificates, we need documentation to show your physical presence in the US. Good morning Mr. Brown,

In the case you are referencing to, a power of attorney is unacceptable. The form in question is an affirmation, under oath, that the child being documented is the biological child of the parent and that the parent is swearing to support the child until the age of 18.  In this case, the document very specifically states at the top of the document that it must be signed and affirmed in front of a notary, consular officer or other official who can legally witness affirmations.  I see that you were able to get one half of the form notarized, however it is the top half that was missing and needed to be notarized.  Please let us know if you have any further questions about the requirements of this process.

Additionally, I am glad you emailed because there is one more document that I forgot to add to the list needed to complete your children's birth certificates, we need documentation to show your physical presence in the US.  The only document I have is your military service which is only 2 of the 5 years needed.  The best document is a school record, however it does not need to be a school record, but that is usually the easiest to get. Others might include licenses, rental agreements, mortgage documents, employment records, utility bills. Again, please let us know if you need clarification on this requirement. This document can be scanned and emailed.

Thank you for your inquiry, we look forward to your response.
