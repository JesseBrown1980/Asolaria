Yes, keep this newsletter By Amber Phillips President Donald Trump, for years, has railed against mail-in voting, saying he lost the 2020 election because of it. While he has called it fraud, there’s a real-world explanation: Casting ballots by mail was the norm in only a handful of states before 2020, but it became the predominant way of voting that year because of the pandemic. And mailed ballots take longer to count. So on election night in 2020, Trump was leading in some key swing states, but in the days after, as mailed ballots were counted, Joe Biden was declared the winner of the presidential election. Now that he’s back in office, Trump is trying to ban mail voting or, at least, make it much harder to use. “Mail-in voting means mail-in cheating,” Trump said Monday. “I call it mail-in cheating, and we’ve got to do something about it all.” His efforts could peak this week: Republicans in Congress are planning to vote on a provision to severely limit voting by mail as part of a broader bill to change how people vote, and the Supreme Court seems ready to limit voting by mail, too, based on arguments it heard this week. Some or all of this could be in place by the November midterm elections, affecting voters of every party. Here’s what’s going on. Congress is considering limiting who can vote by mail Trump has frequently demanded sweeping changes to election law ahead of November elections when his party could lose control of one or both chambers of Congress. Some of his ideas are blatantly unconstitutional, like saying that Republicans should “take over the voting.” Advertisement But a lot of his ideas have been rolled into legislation called the Save America Act . It would dramatically change how people cast ballots by requiring proof of citizenship to register to vote, photo IDs to vote and anyone voting by mail to show up in person and first provide proof of citizenship, arguably defeating the purpose. (Some disabled voters and active duty troops would be exempt from the new mail-ballot rules.) The bill passed the House and is in the Senate this week, but Democrats are expected to block it with a filibuster. Still, Trump’s focus on mail-in voting has had an impact: A number of conservative states have passed strict voting laws targeting it in recent years. The Supreme Court could soon limit when mailed ballots can be counted The most contentious aspect of voting by mail is when election officials should accept mailed ballots. Most states require ballots to arrive by Election Day. But there are 14 states that allow ballots to arrive days or weeks afterward, as long as the ballot is postmarked by Election Day. Republicans and Trump have argued that practice leads to mistrust and could open the door to fraud (though there is no evidence that ballots arriving after Election Day have been part of vote fraud). Advertisement Conservatives sued the state of Mississippi for its practice of accepting ballots that arrive after Election Day and that case made its way to the Supreme Court this week . “The losers are going to doubt the result, full stop,” attorney Paul D. Clement said in arguments Monday before the court. “That is bad for our system.” It has never been the case that all votes are counted on Election Day; in addition to mailed ballots, there are provisional ballots to be counted. The entire process of counting ballots, checking any errors and having election officials certify them can take weeks, said Tammy Patrick, a former election official in Arizona now with the nonpartisan Election Center. “We have never had official winners on election night,” she said. Conservative justices, who make up a majority of the Supreme Court, suggested it would be easier and cleaner to set Election Day as a deadline for ballots to be received, while liberal justices said it’s up to Congress and states and, so far, Congress hasn’t limited this practice. President Donald Trump, for years, has railed against mail-in voting, saying he lost the 2020 election because of it.

While he has called it fraud, there’s a real-world explanation: Casting ballots by mail was the norm in only a handful of states before 2020, but it became the predominant way of voting that year because of the pandemic.

And mailed ballots take longer to count. So on election night in 2020, Trump was leading in some key swing states, but in the days after, as mailed ballots were counted, Joe Biden was declared the winner of the presidential election.

Now that he’s back in office, Trump is trying to ban mail voting or, at least, make it much harder to use. “Mail-in voting means mail-in cheating,” Trump said Monday. “I call it mail-in cheating, and we’ve got to do something about it all.”

<https://sli.washingtonpost.com/click?s=987489&li=thefix&m=629a290b99188f8d03d630cf68dfeaec&p=69c2e44a715f260743bc4dd5&stpe=default>

<https://sli.washingtonpost.com/click?s=987489&li=thefix&m=629a290b99188f8d03d630cf68dfeaec&p=69c2e44a715f260743bc4dd5&stpe=default>

His efforts could peak this week: Republicans in Congress are planning to vote on a provision to severely limit voting by mail as part of a broader bill to change how people vote, and the Supreme Court seems ready to limit  <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/4719363/69c2e44a715f260743bc4dd5/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/15/47/69c2e44a715f260743bc4dd5>voting by mail, too, based on arguments it heard this week.

Some or all of this could be in place by the November midterm elections, affecting voters of every party.

Here’s what’s going on.
Congress is considering limiting who can vote by mail
Trump has frequently demanded sweeping changes to election law ahead of November elections when his party could lose control of one or both chambers of Congress. Some of his ideas are blatantly unconstitutional, like saying that Republicans should “take over the voting.”

But a lot of his ideas have been rolled into legislation called the Save America Act <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/4719360/69c2e44a715f260743bc4dd5/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/17/47/69c2e44a715f260743bc4dd5>. It would dramatically change how people cast ballots by requiring proof of citizenship to register to vote, photo IDs to vote and anyone voting by mail to show up in person and first provide proof of citizenship, arguably defeating the purpose. (Some disabled voters and active duty troops would be exempt from the new mail-ballot rules.)

The bill passed the House and is in the Senate this week, but Democrats are expected to block it with a filibuster. Still, Trump’s focus on mail-in voting has had an impact: A number of conservative states have passed strict voting laws targeting it in recent years.
The Supreme Court could soon limit when mailed ballots can be counted
The most contentious aspect of voting by mail is when election officials should accept mailed ballots. Most states require ballots to arrive by Election Day. But there are 14 states that allow ballots to arrive days or weeks afterward, as long as the ballot is postmarked by Election Day.

Republicans and Trump have argued that practice leads to mistrust and could open the door to fraud (though there is no evidence <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/471bb30/69c2e44a715f260743bc4dd5/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/18/47/69c2e44a715f260743bc4dd5> that ballots arriving after Election Day have been part of vote fraud).

Conservatives sued the state of Mississippi for its practice of accepting ballots that arrive after Election Day and that case made its way to the Supreme Court this week <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/4719363/69c2e44a715f260743bc4dd5/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/20/47/69c2e44a715f260743bc4dd5>. “The losers are going to doubt the result, full stop,” attorney Paul D. Clement said in arguments Monday before the court. “That is bad for our system.”

It has never been the case that all votes are counted on Election Day; in addition to mailed ballots, there are provisional ballots to be counted. The entire process of counting ballots, checking any errors and having election officials certify them can take weeks, said Tammy Patrick, a former election official in Arizona now with the nonpartisan Election Center. “We have never had official winners on election night,” she said.

Conservative justices, who make up a majority of the Supreme Court, suggested it would be easier and cleaner to set Election Day as a deadline for ballots to be received, while liberal justices said it’s up to Congress and states and, so far, Congress hasn’t limited this practice.

Democrats and critics of Trump have argued that limiting what ballots can be counted are part of Trump’s widespread efforts to meddle <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/466c698/69c2e44a715f260743bc4dd5/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/21/47/69c2e44a715f260743bc4dd5> in the midterm elections.

“It’s simple,” writes Democratic election lawyer Marc Elias in his newsletter Democracy Docket, “Republicans are trying to make it harder for your mail-in ballot to count.”

Whatever the court decides, the changes could be in place by the November elections, which could force states to dramatically change their policies, as voting in primaries is already underway for the midterms.

“I think any kind of disruption of election practices and rules, any introduction of chaos, can create a pretext for people who are trying to undermine elections,” said Wendy Weiser, with the Brennan Center for Justice, a nonpartisan think tank that focuses on democracy issues and filed a brief in this case in support of military voters who vote absentee.
Experts want you to know that voting by mail is safe and secure
Mail-in voting is used in some form in almost every state, at the very least for military service members. It remained popular after the pandemic; a study found nearly 1 in 3 Americans <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/471bb31/69c2e44a715f260743bc4dd5/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/22/47/69c2e44a715f260743bc4dd5> voted by mail in 2024. Eight states conduct their elections almost entirely by mail. Astronauts in space have voted <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/3f048b3/66e9f7ea38ca915e662ba477/596a0e109bbc0f6d71c91171/14/47/66e9f7ea38ca915e662ba477?utm_campaign=wp_the_5_minute_fix&utm_medium=email&utm_source=newsletter> by absentee.

Trump just voted by mail <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/471bb32/69c2e44a715f260743bc4dd5/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/24/47/69c2e44a715f260743bc4dd5> for a special election in Florida despite being in Florida over the weekend, The Washington Post found.

“We’ve had mail voting since the Civil War, and we know it’s secure,” said David Becker, a former Justice Department voting rights attorney and head of the nonpartisan Center for Election Innovation & Research.

Many Republicans like it, too.

“Over 70 percent of Montanans vote mail-in, and it goes up every election,” Sen. Steve Daines (R-Montana) told reporters <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/4709353/69c2e44a715f260743bc4dd5/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/25/47/69c2e44a715f260743bc4dd5> last week.

“The president is wildly overplaying his hand,” Matt Germer, the director of the governance program at the R Street Institute, a conservative think tank, told me last year when Trump first proposed banning mail voting <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/443eb2a/69c2e44a715f260743bc4dd5/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/26/47/69c2e44a715f260743bc4dd5>. “There are trade-offs to voting by mail but by and large it is a secure method for voting, and it is also a necessary part of our voting system.”
