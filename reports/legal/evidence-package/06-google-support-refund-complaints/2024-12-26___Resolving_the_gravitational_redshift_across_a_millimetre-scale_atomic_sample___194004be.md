*Dr. Tobias Bothwell, Dr. Jun Ye, and Co-Authors* JILA, National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) and University of Colorado 440 UCB, Boulder, Colorado, USA *Subject:* Potential Collaboration on Gravitational Redshift and Discrete Physics Frameworks Dear Dr. Bothwell, Dr. Ye, and esteemed colleagues, I was captivated by your recent work, *"Resolving the gravitational redshift across a millimetre-scale atomic sample,"* published in *Nature*. Your remarkable achievement of measuring the gravitational redshift on such a fine spatial scale is a groundbreaking step towards uniting quantum mechanics and general relativity. The precision of your clock measurements and their implications for understanding quantum systems in curved spacetime are particularly inspiring. As a researcher focused on discrete digital physics, I have developed a framework where spacetime is modeled as a sequence of discrete frames, and subparticles carry metatags that encode their properties and interactions. In my recent paper, *"Integration and Refinement of Digital Physics: Unifying Quantum and Classical with a Discrete Framework,"* I propose that such a model can naturally account for phenomena like gravitational redshift and its relationship with quantum mechanics. Specifically, within this framework: - Gravitational effects could be interpreted as metadata-driven adjustments to the rendering of discrete spacetime frames, with each frame capturing variations in gravitational potential. - The wavefunction of quantum objects oscillating in curved spacetime may manifest as metatag perturbations influencing local frame transitions, offering a new computational lens on your experimental findings. Your precise measurements and the challenges of intra-sample corrections align perfectly with the opportunities afforded by a discrete physics model. It would be a privilege to explore whether your data could be analyzed or modeled through this lens, potentially yielding novel insights into the intersection of quantum mechanics and general relativity. Additionally, your expertise in ultracold atomic systems provides an invaluable experimental counterpart to the computational and theoretical approaches I am developing. I would greatly value the opportunity to discuss these ideas with you and explore avenues for collaboration. Please let me know a time that might be convenient for a call or meeting. I am also happy to share my paper and further details about the theoretical framework to facilitate our discussion. Thank you for your pioneering contributions to this field, and I hope this letter initiates an engaging and fruitful exchange of ideas. Dear Dr. Bothwell, Dr. Ye, and esteemed colleagues,

I was captivated by your recent work, *"Resolving the gravitational
redshift across a millimetre-scale atomic sample,"* published in *Nature*.
Your remarkable achievement of measuring the gravitational redshift on such
a fine spatial scale is a groundbreaking step towards uniting quantum
mechanics and general relativity. The precision of your clock measurements
and their implications for understanding quantum systems in curved
spacetime are particularly inspiring.

As a researcher focused on discrete digital physics, I have developed a
framework where spacetime is modeled as a sequence of discrete frames, and
subparticles carry metatags that encode their properties and interactions.
In my recent paper, *"Integration and Refinement of Digital Physics:
Unifying Quantum and Classical with a Discrete Framework,"* I propose that
such a model can naturally account for phenomena like gravitational
redshift and its relationship with quantum mechanics. Specifically, within
this framework:

   - Gravitational effects could be interpreted as metadata-driven
   adjustments to the rendering of discrete spacetime frames, with each frame
   capturing variations in gravitational potential.
   - The wavefunction of quantum objects oscillating in curved spacetime
   may manifest as metatag perturbations influencing local frame transitions,
   offering a new computational lens on your experimental findings.

Your precise measurements and the challenges of intra-sample corrections
align perfectly with the opportunities afforded by a discrete physics
model. It would be a privilege to explore whether your data could be
analyzed or modeled through this lens, potentially yielding novel insights
into the intersection of quantum mechanics and general relativity.
Additionally, your expertise in ultracold atomic systems provides an
invaluable experimental counterpart to the computational and theoretical
approaches I am developing.

I would greatly value the opportunity to discuss these ideas with you and
explore avenues for collaboration. Please let me know a time that might be
convenient for a call or meeting. I am also happy to share my paper and
further details about the theoretical framework to facilitate our
discussion.

Thank you for your pioneering contributions to this field, and I hope this
letter initiates an engaging and fruitful exchange of ideas.

Warm regards,
Jesse Daniel Brown PhD
