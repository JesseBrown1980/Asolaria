Yes, keep this newsletter By Amber Phillips As President Donald Trump pushes for an end to the war in Iran, the U.S. military has fired hundreds of high-tech missiles at the country in recent weeks and there’s evidence it has laid land mines in residential areas of Iran, a practice the military hasn’t used in decades. Here’s what else happened under Trump this week. Pay for TSA workers is up in the air The Senate passed a late-night agreement that could untangle airports from the Department of Homeland Security shutdown, but the agreement failed in the House Friday afternoon. Senators approved a bill to fund the department minus Immigration and Customs Enforcement, which would give Transportation Security Administration workers a paycheck again. (Some airport security workers have stopped showing up to work after a month of no paychecks.) House Republicans, though, were angry that it did not include the Save America Act — a sweeping voting bill championed by Trump — and that it did not appropriate money for the administration’s deportation efforts. In the Senate deal, Democrats didn’t get any of the restrictions on ICE agents that they had been seeking, such as barring them from wearing masks and requiring them to obtain judicial warrants before entering private property. And ICE has been operating this whole time. Republicans already infused ICE with billions of dollars and say they plan to do more. “What’s coming next is going to supercharge deportations,” Sen. Eric Schmitt (R-Missouri) said on the Senate floor. Republicans lost an election in Trump’s backyard A majority of voters in Trump’s Palm Beach neighborhood voted this week for a Democrat, Emily Gregory, to represent them in Florida’s state Senate. The results of this special election were surprising, since the district leans Republican. Advertisement It’s also a feel-good win for Democrats. “Trump’s neighbors rejected his brand of politics in a special election, and the president will be represented by a Democrat in the Florida House,” The Washington Post’s Dan Merica and Marianna Sotomayor point out . Trump voted in the election (by mail despite saying it’s “cheating.”). It could be more than symbolic, though. There’s evidence some Republicans in the election voted for Gregory. And across the country, Democrats have won a dozen special elections for state legislative races. In November, they have a chance at taking back not just Congress but also several state legislative chambers , including Arizona, Michigan and Wisconsin. He also left the White House with another record so sensitive that only six high-ranking government officials had access to it. That’s according to the Justice Department’s investigation several years ago of how Trump handled classified documents when he was no longer president. It never did. After a stunning FBI search of Mar-a-Lago, Trump was charged with mishandling classified information. The charges were dropped by a Trump-appointed judge who issued a controversial ruling about how Smith was appointed. The case largely lives on in words now. Trump has maintained his innocence and accused the Justice Department under Joe Biden of political persecution. This past weekend, Trump installed a statute of Christopher Columbus on White House grounds, and his administration announced Trump’s signature will appear on U.S. dollars soon, something no other president has done. As President Donald Trump pushes for an end to the war in Iran, the U.S. military has fired hundreds of high-tech missiles <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/4723126/69c6e1c6715f260743bd20c1/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/13/50/69c6e1c6715f260743bd20c1> at the country in recent weeks and there’s evidence it has laid land mines <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/4723127/69c6e1c6715f260743bd20c1/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/14/50/69c6e1c6715f260743bd20c1> in residential areas of Iran, a practice the military hasn’t used in decades.

Here’s what else happened under Trump this week.
Pay for TSA workers is up in the air
The Senate passed a late-night agreement that could untangle airports from the Department of Homeland Security shutdown, but the agreement failed in the House <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/472312f/69c6e1c6715f260743bd20c1/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/15/50/69c6e1c6715f260743bd20c1> Friday afternoon.

<https://sli.washingtonpost.com/click?s=987489&li=thefix&m=629a290b99188f8d03d630cf68dfeaec&p=69c6e1c6715f260743bd20c1&stpe=default>

<https://sli.washingtonpost.com/click?s=987489&li=thefix&m=629a290b99188f8d03d630cf68dfeaec&p=69c6e1c6715f260743bd20c1&stpe=default>

 Senators approved a bill to fund the department minus Immigration and Customs Enforcement, which would give Transportation Security Administration workers a paycheck again. (Some airport security workers have stopped showing up to work after a month of no paychecks.)

House Republicans, though, were angry that it did not include the Save America Act — a sweeping voting bill championed by  Trump — and that it did not appropriate money for the administration’s deportation efforts.

In the Senate deal, Democrats didn’t get any of the restrictions on ICE agents that they had been seeking, such as barring them from wearing masks and requiring them to obtain judicial warrants before entering private property.

And ICE has been operating this whole time. Republicans already infused ICE with billions of dollars and say they plan to do more. “What’s coming next is going to supercharge deportations,” Sen. Eric Schmitt (R-Missouri) said on the Senate floor.
Republicans lost an election in Trump’s backyard
A majority of voters in Trump’s Palm Beach neighborhood voted this week for a Democrat, Emily Gregory, to represent them in Florida’s state Senate. The results of this special election were surprising, since the district leans Republican.

It’s also a feel-good win for Democrats. “Trump’s neighbors rejected his brand of politics in a special election, and the president will be represented by a Democrat in the Florida House,” The Washington Post’s Dan Merica and Marianna Sotomayor point out <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/4723129/69c6e1c6715f260743bd20c1/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/19/50/69c6e1c6715f260743bd20c1>. Trump voted in the election (by mail  <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/471bb32/69c6e1c6715f260743bd20c1/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/20/50/69c6e1c6715f260743bd20c1>despite saying it’s “cheating.”).

It could be more than symbolic, though. There’s evidence some Republicans in the election voted for Gregory. And across the country, Democrats have won a dozen special elections for state legislative races. In November, they have a chance at taking back not just Congress but also several state legislative chambers <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/472312a/69c6e1c6715f260743bd20c1/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/21/50/69c6e1c6715f260743bd20c1>, including Arizona, Michigan and Wisconsin. Based on what happened in Florida this week, some Democrats who work on statehouse races predicted to Dan and Marianna “the largest gain for Democrats in over two decades.”
Trump showed a classified map on a plane, according to a prosecution memo
While he was out of office in 2022, Trump was on a private plane and allegedly showed a classified map to passengers <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/471e6b8/69c6e1c6715f260743bd20c1/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/22/50/69c6e1c6715f260743bd20c1>. He also left the White House with another record so sensitive that only six high-ranking government officials had access to it.

That’s according to the Justice Department’s investigation several years ago of how Trump handled classified documents when he was no longer president.

This came to light this week because Republicans in Congress are investigating special counsel Jack Smith and unearthed this memo detailing how Smith planned to prosecute Trump if the case went to trial.

It never did. After a stunning FBI search of Mar-a-Lago, Trump was charged with mishandling classified information. The charges were dropped by a Trump-appointed judge who issued a controversial ruling about how Smith was appointed.

The case largely lives on in words now. Trump has maintained his innocence and accused the Justice Department under Joe Biden of political persecution.

Smith told Congress in January he would have secured a conviction: “No one should be above the law in our country, and the law required that he be held to account,” he said. “So that is what I did.”
Trump keeps renovating much of Washington and putting his name on things
Trump hasn’t just knocked down the East Wing to make way for a massive ballroom. He’s also redecorated much of the White House in gold, paved over some of the Rose Garden, added insulting descriptions of presidents he doesn’t like and is considering turning a historic treaty room into a guest room <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/472312b/69c6e1c6715f260743bd20c1/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/24/50/69c6e1c6715f260743bd20c1>, the New York Times reported this week.

This past weekend, Trump installed <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/4719366/69c6e1c6715f260743bd20c1/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/25/50/69c6e1c6715f260743bd20c1> a statute of Christopher Columbus on White House grounds, and his administration announced <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/472312c/69c6e1c6715f260743bd20c1/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/26/50/69c6e1c6715f260743bd20c1> Trump’s signature will appear on U.S. dollars soon, something no other president has done. He could also be pictured <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/470df8d/69c6e1c6715f260743bd20c1/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/27/50/69c6e1c6715f260743bd20c1> on a 24-carat gold coin despite a bipartisan commission saying it reeks of subservience to royalty, reports The Post’s Dan Diamond.

Last month, Trump announced he’s renovating the Kennedy Center <https://s2.washingtonpost.com/472312d/69c6e1c6715f260743bd20c1/60395ca19bbc0f23b15847a7/28/50/69c6e1c6715f260743bd20c1> after he put his name on the front, sparking fears he could demolish it.

There are court challenges to some of the major renovations, but they’re moving slowly: Preservationists sued this week to stop Kennedy Center renovations without congressional approval. Trump’s ballroom is facing a similar court challenge, but Trump is racing to start construction by April, and it could be approved by an arts commission full of his allies next week.

“President Trump is the builder-in-chief with an extraordinary eye for detail and design, and his bold vision will be imprinted upon the fabric of America and be felt by generations to come,” Davis Ingle, a White House spokesman, told the Times. “His successes will continue to give the White House the glory it deserves.”
