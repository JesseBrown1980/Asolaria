Thank you for the clarification. But you are wrong. The service asked for is covered by the power of attorny as an affiant. The affiant can and does have the power to sign all documentation in the name of and in the absence of the power of attorney provider. Clearly it is stated. Hague Convention specifies the modalities through which a document issued in one of the signatory countries can be certified for legal purposes in all the other signatory states. In other words, Hague Convention provides with validity and with effectiveness to foreign legal documents in other signatory countries apart from their home legislation and sets forth the rules for such. Such a certification is called an apostille. It is an international certification comparable to notarization process in domestic laws. It should significantly be noted that an apostille does not give information regarding the quality of the document, but certifies the signature and correctness of the seal/stamp on the document which is actually necessary to be certified. After apostillization process, the document will enjoy the legal effect that it originally has in its origin legislation, in the receiving country. Under the affiant rule, you will recognize my wife as myself, and any forms that I need to sign or submit will be recognized as being signed in my absence. An affiant does not need to be the person himself or herself, it just means that a person who represents the validity of the information os infact authorized to do so in the name of the person. Please recognize this. Second, I was born in the United States of America and should not need to prove residency. Since you see my birth certificate, school grades, and military service, this is just proof of presence that you are looking at, yet it has no effective influence on the qualification of my children as both my parents are also native born Americans and I lived in the usa for over 36 years. Please let me know if I will need a lawyer about the validity of a power of attorny in an affiant situation at your embassy, as a the other embassies recognize this form of service.
