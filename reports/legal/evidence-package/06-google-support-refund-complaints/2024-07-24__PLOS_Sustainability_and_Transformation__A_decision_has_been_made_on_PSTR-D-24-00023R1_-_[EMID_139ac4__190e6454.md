Reviewer #4: This paper aims to introduce a new class of strategic management methods, Systemic Management Innovations (SMIs), designed to aid organizations&#39; leaders and scholars by distinguishing approaches, tools, and methods that are adequately sophisticated for emerging complexity.&quot; The manuscript proposes to address the sustainability challenges municipalities face by introducing Systemic Management Innovations (SMIs). These innovations are characterized by their integration of systems theory and strategic management to provide practical insights and techniques for leaders. The present complex and volatile contexts call for novel management approaches, and this research can contribute to that aim. However, from the reviewer perspective, the manuscript needs a clear research focus, with excessive text and the absence of a well-developed discussion and conclusion section. Please see the following comments for further details. C1. Please clearly define the concepts of sustainability and local government with the support of robust academic references. C2. The authors could present more arguments for adopting sustainability considerations within city governments. See the following non-mandator suggestions: &bull; According to the recent SDGs Report (UN, 2022), humanity&#39;s current situation is alarming, and the window to avoid a climate catastrophe is rapidly closing (https://www.un.org/development/desa/dspd/2022/07/sdgs-report/). &bull; Academic research (Leal Filho W, Viera Trevisan La&iacute;, Simon Rampasso I, Anholon R, Pimenta Dinis MA, Londero Brandli L, Sierra J, Lange Salvia A, Pretorius R, Nicolau M, Paulino Pires Eustachio Jo&atilde;o Henrique, Mazutti J, When the alarm bells ring: Why the UN sustainable development goals may not be achieved by 2030, Journal of Cleaner Production (2023), doi: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2023.137108)) emphasizes stronger action to avoid significant challenges such as a climate catastrophe. C3. The authors claim to have performed a literature review and cited significant literature throughout the manuscript. Hence, please present details of the literature review: what databases were searched, and what keywords were applied? How many articles were identified? What were the inclusion and exclusion criteria? What are the points of agreement, disagreement and research gaps? C4. Moreover (see section 3), frameworks are not theories, and no details are present concerning the consulted representatives from local cities, the core team members, the two Canadian cities, the interviews, and the reflexive groups. It would be valuable to present enough detail to scientifically support the research and allow another researcher to apply these methods to achieve similar results. C5. Theoretical support is needed to predict and explain the nature of relationships between phenomena based on a systemized structure and generalize the research findings. Hence, the author(s) should support their research with valid management theories (e.g., Porter Industry Theory, 1980, 1985: Freeman&#39;s Stakeholder Theory, 1984;, the Resource-Based View (RBV) Theory, Barney, 1991, 2001; or the Institutional Theory, DiMaggio & Powell, 2000). C6. The authors should refer to existing business models, such as the EFQM 2020 that adopt a systems perspective, integrate sustainability within the organization&#39;s purpose and strategy (e.g., by aligning with the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), encompassing digital transformation and aim to simultaneously deliver performance and ensure transformation, creating enduring value for its key stakeholders and achieving remarkable results (Fonseca, L. (2022). The EFQM 2020 model. A theoretical and critical review. Total Quality Management & Business Excellence, 33:9-10, 1011-1038, DOI: 10.1080/14783363.2021.1915121). C7. From the reviewer&#39;s point of view, this manuscript should be considerably streamlined and improved to enhance its academic, policy, and business value. The manuscript introduces a promising approach to addressing municipal sustainability challenges through Systemic Management Innovations. However, it would benefit from more specific details, evidence of success, and practical guidance to strengthen its appeal and feasibility. By addressing these areas, the proposal could more convincingly argue that SMIs are the crucial missing link for municipalities seeking sustainability and resilience.
