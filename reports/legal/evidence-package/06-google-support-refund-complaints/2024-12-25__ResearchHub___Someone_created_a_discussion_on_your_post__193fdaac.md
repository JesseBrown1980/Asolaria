=== HTML PART === Your Notifications María José Guzmán Monsalve has replied to your comment: Alternate EoS for Finite Density Black Holes in a Quantum Gravity Framework • --> Dec. 25, 2024, 11:54 a.m. Dear Jesse, Thanks for your thoughtful reply and the references, some of them written by colleagues that I know in person or have papers in common. Although it is impossible for me to thoroughly review all the references that you have cited, I have plenty of experience solving the equations of motion for modified theories of gravity. What I know for certain, is that it is not enough with proposing a matter source (scalar field, anisotropic matter) with a smooth behavior in certain coordinates in order to claim that you have got rid of singularities. In some cases, it is enough with just modifying gravity in order to find black-hole-like solutions in vacuum, or to modify Schwarzschild solution. One example of the former is https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11843, and an example of the latter is https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04087, although there are thousands of papers available online that have found success in this important attempt. One of your references https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.02280 has found the time dynamics of the scalar field after Einstein&#x27;s equations have been solved, by assuming homogeneity and isotropy, which is different from your case since you want to find a black-hole solution. Also, you have proposed the solution before finding the metric appropriate for that, that solves the equations of motion. You wrongly assume that by smoothing the scalar field will always get rid of the singularity in the metric, but this is not the case. For instance, in f(R) gravity, although it depends on the function, it is mostly found a divergence in the Kretschmann scalar which unavoidable points to a physical singularity: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.14141 In your response you have not addressed my claim of the lack of expertise in tensor calculus, that would make your idea a potentially scientifically sounding one. However, in the way you have posed the methodology, and due to the lack of proper solving for the spacetime metric, I do not consider this work addresses or solves the problems it claims to solve. Best wishes, Maria View Unsubscribe or change how frequently I get updated
